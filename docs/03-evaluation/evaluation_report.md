# Evaluation dossier

Numbers below come from run `46cb3732ab4d` (full profile, seed 42). Every table is regenerated by
`make all` into `results.md`; raw outputs are in `docs/03-evaluation/run/`. If you change code or
config, rerun and update the numbers quoted here.

## 1. Decision

**GO, with one documented exception.** 14 of 15 acceptance gates pass. The one miss is interval
coverage at 85.6% against an 75 to 85% band: the error is on the safe side (intervals slightly too
wide) and comes from integer-valued intervals on slow-moving items (section 5).

Baselines: **B0** seasonal naive (same weekday last week) and **B1** a planner-style smoothed level x
weekday index with negative-binomial intervals. Inventory baseline **P0** is B1 x (lead time + review)
x 1.2.

## 3. Accuracy

| Model | WAPE | MASE | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0 seasonal naive | 0.595 | 1.154 | +2.8% |
| B1 planner | 0.481 | 0.987 | +0.7% |
| M1 LightGBM, sales only | 0.492 | 1.052 | +0.9% |
| M2 LightGBM, multisource | 0.409 | 0.947 | -0.6% |
| **SenseCast (routed)** | **0.407** | **0.895** | -1.1% |

* **31.6% lower WAPE than seasonal naive**, 15.5% lower than the planner baseline.
* M1 (machine learning without new data) is no better than a good smoothing baseline. The gain comes
  from **the new signals, not from the algorithm**: M2 vs M1 is -18.2% WAPE on festival and heavy-rain
  days and -16.9% overall.
* The gain holds in every store (WAPE 0.375 to 0.429 vs B0 0.566 to 0.623) and every day type
  (festival 0.413, heavy rain 0.410, promotion 0.391, normal 0.399).
* Store-level MASE 0.933: the routed item forecasts, summed to store-day, beat the store's own weekly naive.

**Intermittent demand.** The router picked SBA for intermittent and TSB for lumpy items on the
calibration block. On test, the routed MASE for these classes is 0.997 vs 1.093 for B0 and 1.034 for
M2: for slow movers, the simple specialised method beats the global model.

**Cold start (negative result, kept on purpose).** For the first 28 days of a new item, the global
model M2 (WAPE 0.477) beat the analog-item method C1 (1.007) and the category mean (1.121). C1 was
chosen as the innovation in the blueprint; the data said otherwise. Attribute similarity (price, pack
size) says little about how fast a new item sells, and the global model already uses the item's age
and the store-category context. The calibration block chose M2, so production uses M2.

## 4. Demand sensing: does the model recover the true effects?

`figures/effect_recovery.png` compares what M2 learned (partial dependence at horizons 1 to 2) with
the effect planted in the generator.

* **Rain on monsoon items:** the shape is right (steep rise up to ~10 mm, then flattening) and the
  first 2 mm are recovered almost exactly (+43% vs +47%). Heavy-rain effects are under-estimated
  (+107% vs +366% at 80 mm).
* **Heat on beverages:** direction right, size much smaller (+9% vs +82% from 27 to 37 degC).

Why the attenuation, and why it is expected: (1) the model sees a weather **forecast**, not the
weather, and noisy inputs shrink estimated effects (regression dilution); (2) temperature moves
slowly, so recent-sales features already carry most of its effect; the weather feature only adds the
day-to-day part; (3) the test block is the monsoon, where 35 degC days never occur, so that part of
the curve is extrapolation. The practical reading: the system senses weather changes correctly in
direction and timing, but a planner should not read the drivers chart as a causal elasticity.

## 5. Uncertainty

| Forecast | Pinball (mean of P10/P50/P90) | P10 to P90 coverage |
|---|---|---|
| B1 negative-binomial | 1.714 | 83.5% |
| SenseCast raw quantiles | 1.478 | 84.8% |
| SenseCast + conformal (CQR) | **1.477** | 85.6% |

Pinball loss is 13.8% better than the planner's intervals. Coverage by class: smooth 81.1%,
erratic 77.8%, lumpy 84.2%, new 90.7%, **intermittent 94.8%**. For an item that sells 0 or 1 unit a
day, the tightest honest interval is [0, 1], which already contains ~95% of days; no integer interval
can hit exactly 80%. This is what pushes the overall figure to 85.6%. Options for a later release:
randomised (PIT-based) intervals for count data, or reporting coverage only for classes where it is
meaningful. We kept the gate as frozen and report the miss.

## 6. Hierarchy

| Level | Base | Bottom-up | MinT-shrink | **WLS (served)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| total | 0.073 | 0.076 | 0.069 | **0.069** |
| region | 0.099 | 0.089 | 0.087 | **0.086** |
| store | 0.106 | 0.099 | 0.097 | **0.096** |
| store x category | 0.190 | 0.170 | 0.175 | **0.170** |
| item x store | 0.427 | 0.427 | 0.438 | **0.426** |

Independent base forecasts disagreed by up to 4,163 units per day across levels; after reconciliation
the error is 0. **WLS with variance scaling** is served. Full MinT needs a 1,554 x 1,554 error
covariance estimated from 112 calibration cases; even with shrinkage (lambda = 0.51) it hurt item-level
accuracy. WLS uses only the variances and was as good or better at every level. We fixed WLS in the
config on this statistical argument; the test results confirm it but did not choose it.

## 7. Decisions: inventory simulation

1,440 established item-stores, 56 days, 100 demand paths drawn from the TRUE demand distribution,
lead time 3 days, daily review, lost sales.

| Policy | Fill rate | Stockout days | Days of cover | Overstock units/day | Cost INR/day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 manual (B1, +20% buffer) | 91.0% | 7.7% | 1.83 | 1 | 110,482 |
| P1 point ML + residual safety stock | 95.1% | 2.8% | 2.23 | 62 | 69,839 |
| **P2 uncertainty-aware** | **96.2%** | 3.0% | 2.38 | 6 | **50,593** |

Comparing default settings is unfair because they hold different amounts of stock, so each policy's
knob was swept (`figures/simulation.png`). **To reach a 95% fill rate, P0 needs 3.23 days of cover and
P2 needs 1.96: 39% less inventory for the same service.** P2 also dominates P1 at every inventory
level: it places safety stock where the calibrated band is wide (festival weeks, erratic items)
instead of spreading one historical error everywhere, which is why its overstock is a tenth of P1's.
Cost is holding cost at ~25% a year plus 25% margin lost on unmet demand.

## 8. Robustness and failure modes

| Experiment | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Leakage probe: scramble every value after the origin | 0 of 36 features changed | pass |
| Weather feed down for the whole test block | WAPE 0.409 to 0.466 (+5.7 pts); all 167,100 rows flagged; still better than sales-only M1 (0.492) | pass (graceful). The blueprint expected at most 3 pts; the larger loss is itself evidence of how much weather matters in the monsoon |
| Search bot spike (snacks pinned at 100 for 2 days before each origin) | forecasts moved 3.0%, a last-value feature would have jumped 26% | pass |
| Censoring treatment (ignore / drop / impute) | bias vs true demand -8.7% / -10.1% / **-7.6%** | impute least biased, used in production |
| One store loses 20% of demand | bias alert (> 15%) in week 1 | pass |
| Dirty feed | 757 duplicates, 151 negative units, 151 missing prices, 25 future-dated rows quarantined | pass |

**Sales are not demand.** Even the best treatment under-forecasts true demand (7.6% in the ablation;
9.0% overall and 11.9% for the top 10% of items with the production model): sales were censored on 6.5% of item-days by the historical ordering policy, and
the days without a stockout are, by definition, the calmer ones. Imputing a lower bound on stockout
days reduces the bias, it does not remove it. The simulator scores against true demand, so this bias is
already priced into the fill rates above.

## 9. Subgroups and edge cases

* **Stores and regions:** WAPE ranges 0.375 (S08, Nashik) to 0.429 (S02, Mumbai). Mumbai stores are
  hardest (heaviest rain, biggest Ganesh Chaturthi effect). Monitoring raised 2 store-bias alerts
  (S04, opposite signs in weeks 2 and 6), consistent with noise rather than drift.
* **Demand classes:** smooth 0.292, erratic 0.493, new 0.506, lumpy 0.968, intermittent 1.246 (WAPE
  above 1 is normal for items selling a unit every few days; MASE is the fairer lens there).
* **Weekly stability:** WAPE between 0.395 and 0.421 across the 8 test weeks; bias swings from -8%
  (early monsoon weeks) to +8% (mid-August), then back to 0.
* **Forecast stability:** successive forecasts for the same day change by 8.2% (median), vs 87% for B0.

## 10. Monitoring

PSI (train block vs test block) flags six features above 0.2: `temp_fc` and `hum_fc` (a monsoon test
window against a mostly dry-season reference, expected) and the four search features (category
interest genuinely shifted). Calendar and festival features are excluded from PSI because they
follow a known schedule. Weekly WAPE stayed between 0.39 and 0.42 while these alerts fired, so the
right action is a scheduled retrain on recent data, not an emergency. The injected 20% demand drop at
one store was caught by the store-bias alert in the first week.

## 12. Security and privacy checks

All automated, all in CI: RBAC (store managers cannot cross stores, analysts cannot decide, missing
or unknown identities rejected), input validation (IDs, dates, quantities), override guard,
append-only audit (UPDATE and DELETE blocked), CSV formula-injection escaping, rate limiting,
payload cap, `pip-audit`, `gitleaks`, non-root container. No personal data exists in the system.
Threat model: `docs/02-design/architecture.md` section 10.

## 13. Responsible-AI review

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who could be harmed? | Customers (empty shelves), store staff (blamed for model errors), the business (waste). No individual is scored or profiled. |
| Human oversight | Every order is a suggestion. Approve, override (reason required) or reject; every action is logged with the model version. |
| Transparency | Each forecast shows its model (M2, SBA, TSB), calibrated band and the signals moving it. The drivers are associations, not causal elasticities, and the user guide says so. |
| Fairness across groups | Accuracy is reported per store, region, category and demand class. Slow-moving and new items get worse accuracy by nature; they are routed to specialised methods and flagged in the UI. |
| Failure behaviour | Weather outage falls back and flags; bad rows are quarantined, never silently fixed; drift and bias alerts in monitoring. |
| Misuse | Override hoarding is visible in the audit log; a 10x guard blocks typos; managers see only their store. |

## 14. Limitations

1. **Synthetic world.** Effects, noise and censoring are realistic in size but chosen by us. The
   numbers show the method works when those effects exist; they do not prove the size of gains on a
   real chain. Rerun the gates on real POS data before any business claim.
2. **Weather** in this run is the synthetic climatology; with network access the Open-Meteo loader
   uses real observations for the same cities.
3. **Search interest** is generated; real Google Trends data is noisier and sampled.
4. **Inventory position** in production assumes no outstanding orders (the feed has receipts but no
   open purchase orders).
5. **Coverage** is conservative for integer slow movers (section 5).
6. **Cold start**: the analog method did not beat the global model; a better item-similarity signal
   (text embeddings of product names, supplier data) is future work.
7. **Authentication** is a hook for a real identity provider, not an identity provider.
